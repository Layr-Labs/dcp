# Direct angular score with a constant malformed fraction

Let N>=2, n=ceil(log2 N), K=2^24 n and B=4096. Acquire exactly K source copies. The tolerated fraction is 61/256, with fixed malformed positions chosen before any private coins and an ideal product marginal on all other positions. Every branch returns one residue modulo N. Use the native Fourier-label instrument in BASE13 Section 3 with its precision cap set to K, followed by X on the row to give the positive phase convention. For an ideal copy, its hidden Fourier label k is uniform modulo N and the row phase is exp(2 pi i k s/N). The label instrument touches only the position system before the row operations. The Section 3 coupling compares the full clean label transcript to this ideal report with discrepancy probability at most 2^-14 over at most K clean-copy calls. On any bad or padded transcript, use the instrument's deterministic rounding convention to report an integer in [0,N), using 0 if the convention reports an invalid result. Every row measurement has sign Z in {-1,1}.

For each copy use an independent uniform b in {0,...,B-1} from twelve fair bits. Put beta=2 pi b/B. Measure the row sign in the beta equatorial axis, with an exactly executable approximation obtained as follows. A rotation diag(1,exp(-i beta)) before X measurement has the desired ideal law, with conditional mean cos(2 pi k s/N-beta). Generate an approximation within 2^-16 in operator norm up to global phase from one-qubit Clifford+T gates. One uniform fixed-cost implementation enumerates all words in H,T and the inverses and all finite rational certification precisions, in increasing total description length. Certify a strict upper bound under 2^-16 on the distance of the induced sign observable from the desired rotation's sign observable after allowing the conservative factor of two. Concretely, accept once the maximum absolute entry difference of the two 2-by-2 sign observables has a certified upper bound below 2^-16. This implies operator-norm difference below 2^-15. Every tested matrix is computed with convergent rational intervals, and the target trigonometric constants have convergent Taylor interval enclosures. Test only finitely many words and precisions at each length and increase the bound until a word is accepted. Projective single-qubit universality of H and T ensures termination for each of the 4096 fixed targets. Thus all generation work, the table, and the lengths of the words have absolute finite bounds independent of N and of input outcomes. The controller generates and pays for these circuits once and executes the selected word followed by the ordinary X readout. This uses no data-dependent synthesis and no advice. A reference for the finite single-qubit gate approximation principle is Dawson and Nielsen, *The Solovay-Kitaev algorithm*, arXiv:quant-ph/0505030.

Store each record (k,b,Z), discarding both source registers. Given a candidate a in [0,N), define its term to be

    Z * sign_+(cos(2 pi (ka mod N)/N - beta)),

where sign_+(x) returns +1 when x>=0, and -1 otherwise. This is an exact discrete function of three integers. Take u=(B*(ka mod N)-b*N) modulo BN in [0,BN), then test 4u<= BN or 4u>=3BN for the positive sign, including both endpoints. Cross products here take O(n) bits, and the multiplications other than ka are by a fixed word or a fixed-length b. No numerical cosine is evaluated by the scorer. Use normalized average of the K terms and mark iff it is at least 31/128.

## Clean means and simultaneous concentration

For fixed angles x=2 pi k s/N and y=2 pi k a/N, the continuously averaged ideal term is

    (1/(2 pi)) integral_0^(2 pi) cos(x-beta) sign_+(cos(y-beta)) d beta
      = (2/pi) cos(x-y).

Indeed shift beta by y, expand the cosine addition identity, and use the zero sine integral by reflection. The remaining integral of |cos| is 4. On the public grid, compare the equally spaced left-endpoint sum to this integral using B equal half-open cells. Away from the two possible jump locations the integrand is 1-Lipschitz. A jump exactly at a grid endpoint can affect the adjoining cell starting at that endpoint when the tie sign disagrees with the right limit; if it is a right endpoint it does not affect the preceding cell. Thus at most two cells incur an extra worst-case discrepancy of 2/B each. The total discrepancy is at most (2 pi+4)/B. Native measurement synthesis changes the expectation by at most 2^-15, so the combined discrepancy is less than 3/1000 using pi<22/7. Taking the uniform Fourier-label average, the correct candidate has mean at least 2/pi-3/1000 > 633/1000. For an incorrect candidate, the complex geometric sum of exp(2 pi i k(s-a)/N) is zero, so its mean is at most 3/1000. These averages concern the ideal-label experiment with the actual synthesized row circuits.

Fix the malformed set and consider the reduced marginal of the clean systems only. Each local record instrument uses independent private bits and acts only on its own copy. Clean records are independent with the ideal-label law after coupling; no conditioning on malformed records is involved. The original guarantee allows correlations or entanglement with the malformed systems, whose score terms still lie in [-1,1] pointwise. For the ideal-label clean records and any fixed a, Hoeffding gives failure probability at most 2 exp(-K/2097152) for a clean sum differing from its expectation by more than K/1024, because the clean count is at most K and each individual range has length two. The same event holds for all a except with probability 2^(n+1) exp(-8n), less than 1/128 for every n>=1. Add the unconditional label coupling discrepancy 2^-14.

Write f=m/K<=61/256 for the actual contaminated fraction. On that simultaneous event and label coupling, the normalized correct score is at least

    (1-61/256)*(633/1000) - 61/256 - 1/1024 > 31/128.

Every incorrect normalized score is at most

    (1-61/256)*(3/1000) + 61/256 + 1/1024 < 31/128.

Both inequalities are rational. The first lower expression is decreasing as f increases and the second upper expression is increasing as f increases, so the stated endpoint bounds apply uniformly. Thus exactly one valid candidate is marked on the joint good event even for malformed records chosen arbitrarily within the fixed-position promise.

## Counted search

Use an n-qubit uniform candidate register over A=2^n, with padding (a>=N) never marked. Given a register a, compute ka modulo N by the known-multiplier modular addition circuit. Precompute the constants 2^j k mod N by modular doubling, or generate them as needed with counted classical operations; retain O(n^2) bits if needed to emit inverses. For each candidate bit use an O(n)-gate ripple Boolean controlled modular addition and uncomputation. An in-place addition on the valid domain x<N is made reversible by evaluating x+a mod N into a fresh word, exchanging words and clearing the old word with inverse subtraction. Use identity on binary padding and exact Toffoli, CNOT and X. The sign test above uses exact integer comparisons. With the stored sign, update a signed O(log(n+2))-bit sum, then reverse the per-record scratch calculation. After all K terms apply the exact threshold 128 sum >=31 K under the valid-index flag. Apply the sign flip, then reverse the full evaluation including the padding flag. Each record uses O(n^2) gates and core arithmetic operations, with O(n) simultaneous qubits before and after uncomputation. Scan records through classical addresses. A classical postmeasurement mark check uses the same integer rule.

To account for chargeable controller work, use persistent indexed wire and operand cursors in each ripple pass as in BASE13 Section 4.2.1. Each unit cursor increment or decrement uses O(1) amortized inspected and changed bits over a full pass, plus O(log(n+2)) initialization and final boundary terms. Each accessed bit and addressed gate pays O(log(n+2)). A pass has O(n) work before this address factor, and loop setup of O(log(n+2)) address-width bits per pass also fits O(n). Thus one oracle and its emitted inverse cost O(K n^2 log(n+2)) total work including circuit generation. There is no stored circuit of superpolynomial length, and the classical list of known modular constants is at most one record's list.

For the search use a fixed sequence of scales M=1,2,4,... ending at the first power of two with M^2>=16A. At each scale run 128 independent trials, selecting uniform j in [0,M), applying j Grover iterations from the uniform start, and measuring and checking the output. Stop on the first verified mark, or output 0 at the fixed end. One Grover step uses the exact phase oracle and H^n followed by an all-zero sign flip and H^n, with a reusable linear Toffoli chain. For marked fraction p, p>=1/2 succeeds at scale 1 with probability at least 1/2 per trial. For 0<p<1/2 let sin^2(theta)=p. At a scale M, averaging sin^2((2j+1)theta) over uniform j gives 1/2-sin(4M theta)/(4M sin(2theta)), by the finite geometric sum. Since p>=1/A and sin(2theta)>=sqrt(2/A), the first scale M>=2/sin(2theta) is included. At that scale each trial has success probability at least 3/8, so the failure probability is at most (5/8)^128<2^-32. Summing the doubling scales gives O(sqrt(A)) iterations even on an all-failure branch. For p=0 the same cap still applies. No trial calls the source or its inverse.

The bad-event budget 1/128+2^-14+2^-32 is below 1/8. All guarantees are simultaneous. The stored records and controller scratch fit O(n^2) classical bits. The one source copy and native label circuit, candidate register, modular arithmetic and diffusion use O(n) qubits, reused. Native readout on K copies has polynomial work; the charged oracle work times O(2^(n/2)) trials and iterations gives O(2^(n/2) n^3 log2 n) sequential work, with log(n+2) at finite short lengths. K=2^24 n is the exact acquisition budget on every branch. The work growth index is 1, and the other indices are zero. Fraction 61/256 is a supported constant and has no independent time score.
